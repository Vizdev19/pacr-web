03 / Health and safety

Health and safety.

Pacr is not a doctor and does not give medical advice. Training plans, coaching, workout suggestions and voice cues are written partly by AI from what you tell us and from your runs. They can be wrong, and they don't know your health the way a professional does.

  • Check with a doctor before you start a new training programme, especially if you have a heart condition, an injury, are pregnant, or haven't exercised in a while.
  • Stop and get help if you feel pain, dizziness, chest tightness or shortness of breath.
  • Distance, pace, heart rate, elevation, cadence, calories and steps come from your phone's GPS and sensors and from connected devices. They are estimates and can be off.
  • Run where it is safe and legal to do so. Watch traffic, weather and air quality, and don't look at your phone when it isn't safe to.

You run at your own risk, and you decide how hard to push.

04 / Running with others

Running with others.

Squads, clubs, run buddies and events help you run with people you may not know. We don't run background checks on anyone, and we aren't at the runs.

  • Use your judgement before meeting someone: meet in public places, tell someone where you're going, and leave if anything feels wrong.
  • A verified badge means we checked that a club is who it says it is. It is not a promise about any person, run or event.
  • Live run sharing is a convenience, not a safety service. It updates only while your phone has a connection, it notifies nobody, and you must not rely on it in an emergency. In an emergency, call 112 (in India) or your local emergency number.
  • Squad owners and organisers set their own join rules, questions and rules, and can remove members. Those rules sit on top of ours, never instead of them.

If something goes wrong with another runner, report them in the app and, if it's serious, contact the police. We will help where we can — see Grievances.

08 / Event tickets

Event tickets.

Events on Pacr are run by their hosts — clubs and squads — not by Pacr. When you book, your agreement for the event itself is with the host. Pacr provides the listing and the booking, and collects payment on the host's behalf.

Paying

  • Payments are processed by Razorpay. We never see or store your card, UPI or bank details.
  • The price of a paid booking is the host's ticket price, plus a Pacr fee on the tickets (currently 5%), plus GST on that fee (currently 18%). The full breakdown is shown before you pay, and that is the amount you are charged.
  • Your spots are held for ten minutes while you pay. If the hold runs out, they go back on sale.
  • Each seat gets its own ticket and QR code. The host may scan it at the event.

Cancellations and refunds

  • You can't cancel a paid ticket yourself. Only the host can refund a booking, and a refund is always for the whole booking — ticket price and Pacr fee.
  • If the host cancels the event, every paid booking is refunded in full automatically.
  • If you pay but a ticket can't be issued — for example, the event was cancelled while you paid — the payment is refunded in full.
  • Refunds go back to how you paid. Banks usually take 5–7 working days to show them.
  • The host's own refund policy, if they have one, is shown on the event. Ask the host first; if you can't reach them, write to support@pacr.life.

The host is responsible for the event: that it happens as described, that it is safe, and that it has whatever permits and insurance it needs. Pacr isn't responsible for an event that doesn't go as described, but tell us — we can hold a host's payout while we look into it.
